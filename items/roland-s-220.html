Roland Roland S-220

Sampler Module with 2.8" Quickdisk Year: 1987

Description

The Roland S-220 'Digital Sampler' is a 16-voice sampler module in a 19-inch 2U format with a built-in 2.8-inch floppy drive (the Quick Disk format, or QD). The S-220 samples at 12-bit and either 15 or 30kHz, but already has 16-bit A/D converters. The internal memory of 256kB is divided into 4 banks of 64kb each, so all samples combined can be a maximum of 4.4 seconds long, approximately 1.1 seconds per bank. To record a 'long' sample, two banks can be combined. Samples can be edited on the device, equipped with a dynamic envelope, and looped; however, there is no dynamic filter. The S-220 reads QuickDisks from the S-10 and MKS-100, making it data-compatible with the L-100 series. The S-220 operates in MIDI Mono mode, where up to 8 consecutive channels can be used, or up to 4-part multitimbral in Poly mode, where voice distribution is not dynamic but must be pre-selected. In Poly mode, the note range can be limited per channel. Monitor and mouse operation, as with its 'bigger brothers' S-50, S-330, is not provided; the S-220's controls and two-line display must suffice. In February 1987, the S-220 was announced under the name MKS-200, but all models in production were called S-220. Predecessor: MKS-100. Similar: MKS-100, S-330; as keyboard version S-10; more professional and with keyboard S-50. Successor: S-330.

Features

  • 16-voice sampler module
  • 19-inch, 2U rack-mountable format
  • Built-in 2.8-inch Quick Disk floppy drive (QD)
  • 12-bit sampling at 15 or 30kHz
  • 16-bit A/D converters
  • Internal memory of 256kB (4 banks of 64kb each)
  • Maximum sample length of 4.4 seconds (1.1s per bank)
  • Ability to combine two banks for longer samples
  • Samples editable
  • Dynamic envelope
  • Samples loopable
  • No dynamic filter
  • Data compatible with S-10 and MKS-100 (L-100 series)
  • MIDI Mono mode (up to 8 consecutive channels)
  • 4-part multitimbral in Poly mode
  • Note range can be limited per channel in Poly mode
  • Operation via device controls and two-line display (no monitor/mouse support)
