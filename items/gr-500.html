GR-500

Analog Synthesizer Year: 1977

Description

The Roland GR-500 is a polyphonic, analog guitar synthesizer with a 24-pin connection, which can only be controlled with the special GS-500 guitar. On the slanted surface of the box-shaped housing (similar to the Synthesizer Expander 102), the controls are divided into the five sections of the GR-500. To the far left are the common threshold control for the synthesizer's response sensitivity, the phones jack for headphones, the master volume control (Level), and the on/off switch. The first section (G) is for the pure guitar signal, which can only be tapped at the GR-500, not the guitar. A tone control (named EQ Freq), the on/off switch for the EQ, and an output selector switch are the only controls in this section. The GR-500 has three output sum buses, so for each section, you can choose with a switch which sum the signal is routed to. On the back, there are three outputs (CH1 to CH3) and a MIX output, where all three sums are present together. The subsequent section P is called POLYENSEMBLE and converts the signals of all 6 guitar strings into synthesizer signals of the same pitch but without following dynamics. For this, there are sliders for Attack, Decay, and Sustain, which shape the envelope. With appropriate settings, a note struck once on the guitar can sound for up to 5 minutes. The sound of this section can be adjusted with four sliders: F (fundamental = volume of the fundamental tone), L (Low), M (Middle), and H (High) for the low, mid, and high overtones. The subsequent Bass section is monophonic with priority on the last played note. You can choose whether this section reacts to all strings, only the lowest three, or the lowest two strings. The sound is mixed by three sliders: Percussion, Soft, and Hard, with this section being velocity-sensitive and operating in the 16-foot range. Here, too, three envelope sliders are present, with an additional switch to toggle the release time between Short and Long. The main section called SOLO corresponds to a classic, monophonic synthesizer with priority on the last note: VCO with 8-foot sawtooth, 8 and 16-foot square waves with pulse width modulation are mixed and sent through a low-pass filter (VCF, 24dB/Oct) followed by a VCA. The filter can be modulated by LFO, the envelope, and velocity. The control section for an external synthesizer concludes, featuring Portamento and Transpose controls and providing control signals on the rear as CV/Gate signals. The on/off states and volumes for the individual sections are controlled via the corresponding controls of the GS-500 control guitar. The GR-500 is the very first guitar synthesizer and requires a certain degree of playing accuracy and practice to achieve satisfactory results.

Features

  • Polyphonic analog guitar synthesizer
  • 24-pin connection (requires special GS-500 guitar)
  • Five control sections: Guitar, Polyensemble, Bass, Solo, External Synth Control
  • Master Threshold, Phones jack, Master Level controls
  • Guitar section with EQ Freq, EQ on/off, output selector
  • Three output sum buses (CH1-CH3) and a MIX output
  • Polyensemble section: Attack, Decay, Sustain envelope; F/L/M/H overtone sliders; up to 5-minute sustain
  • Monophonic Bass section: last-note priority, string selection, Percussion/Soft/Hard sound mix, velocity-sensitive, 16-foot range, Short/Long release switch
  • Monophonic Solo section: last-note priority, VCO (8-foot sawtooth, 8/16-foot square with PWM), 24dB/Oct Low-Pass Filter (VCF), VCA
  • VCF modulation by LFO, envelope, velocity
  • External Synthesizer control section with Portamento, Transpose, CV/Gate outputs
