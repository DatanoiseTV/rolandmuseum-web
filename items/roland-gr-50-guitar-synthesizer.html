Roland GR-50 Guitar Synthesizer

Guitar/Bass FX Year: 1989

Description

The new Roland GR-50 Guitar Synthesizer is a compact rack-mountable guitar synthesizer system that combines an LA synthesis sound source and a MIDI converter in one powerful device. At the heart of the GR-50 is a super-fast converter circuit that translates the sampled pitch into digital signals. The performance information can be forwarded to the internal sound source, which provides a variety of expressive possibilities. The special feature of this system, however, is the extremely high pitch sampling speed and the high sampling accuracy, which captures even the finest expressive nuances of your guitar style. Playing techniques such as bending and vibrato are reproduced cleanly and smoothly, and the system's high responsiveness allows the guitarist to achieve nuanced techniques like hammer-ons and pull-offs with exceptional transmission accuracy. The GK-2 Synthesizer Driver is the pickup associated with the GR-50 and is available as an optional accessory. It can be easily and effortlessly mounted on any electric guitar or even acoustic guitars (with steel strings). The GR-50's sound source operates with the same Linear-Arithmetic (LA) synthesis technology that forms the basis of Roland's well-known D-series synthesizers. This synthesis method, which has quickly established itself in the professional music scene, produces voices of extraordinary quality and is therefore preferred by top international musicians. The GR-50 offers a choice of various pre-programmed and self-programmable voices, which you can play individually or layer with the normal, natural sound of your guitar. Furthermore, the GR-50 allows polyphonic playing, meaning you can play 12 different synthesizer voices (2 voices per string) simultaneously with your guitar. External MIDI sound sources connected to the MIDI jacks can also be controlled via the GR-50. An external sequencer, such as Roland's Micro Composer MC-500 Mark II, can also be integrated into your system, allowing you to control two of the GR-50's internal LA synthesis voices and a rhythm configuration, while simultaneously playing other internal voices via your guitar. All in all, the GR-50 and GK-2 together form an extremely powerful and flexible guitar synthesizer system that offers the guitarist unmatched responsiveness, comprehensive MIDI control options, all possibilities for polyphonic playing, and first-class sound quality – in short, a system that opens up many new and fascinating expressive possibilities. GR-50 Guitar Synthesizer High Responsiveness GR-50 and GK-2 set new standards for the responsiveness of guitar synthesizer systems. A new large-scale integrated solid-state circuit (LSI chip), specially developed for the GR-50, enables a super-fast signal acquisition algorithm in conjunction with the 16-bit central processing unit (CPU), ensuring high responsiveness and precise pitch recognition. Even fast runs on the lowest string are quickly and reliably detected. The freedom of expression offered by this new guitar synthesizer will seem ideal to every guitarist. In particular, nuanced playing techniques such as hammer-ons and vibrato can now be played without compromising your individual guitar style. In addition to controlling the internal sound sources, the GR-50 also enables simultaneous conversion of all playing data.

Features

  • Rack-mountable guitar synthesizer system
  • LA synthesis sound source
  • MIDI converter
  • Super-fast converter circuit for pitch sampling
  • High pitch sampling speed and accuracy
  • Clean and smooth reproduction of bending and vibrato
  • High responsiveness for hammer-ons and pull-offs
  • Compatible with GK-2 Synthesizer Driver (optional)
  • Based on Roland D-series LA synthesis technology
  • Pre-programmed and self-programmable voices
  • Layering with natural guitar sound
  • Polyphonic playing (12 different synth voices, 2 per string)
  • Control external MIDI sound sources
  • Integration with external sequencers (e.g., Roland MC-500 Mark II)
  • Dedicated LSI chip and 16-bit CPU for fast signal acquisition and precise pitch recognition
